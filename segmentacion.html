Customer Segmentation with K-Means

RFM analysis for marketing strategies

  • Dataset: Online Retail (541,909 initial transactions)
  • Key variables: Recency, Frequency, Monetary
  • 3 clusters identified | Silhouette Score: 0.6458
  • Technologies: Python, scikit-learn, pandas, matplotlib

Project Context

Sales transactions analysis to segment customers using unsupervised techniques (K-Means) based on RFM metrics (Recency, Frequency, Monetary).

Main Variables:

  • InvoiceNo: Transaction number (canceled transactions filtered)
  • StockCode: Product identifier
  • Quantity: Quantity sold per transaction
  • InvoiceDate: Transaction date and time
  • UnitPrice: Unit price of the product
  • CustomerID: Customer identifier (25% missing)
  • Country: Country of the transaction

Detailed Methodology

1. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

  • Filtering canceled transactions and invalid values
  • Identification of outliers in quantities and prices
  • Geographic distribution of sales (73% United Kingdom)
  • Pareto analysis: 25.6% of customers account for 80% of purchases

2. Preprocessing

  • Calculation of RFM metrics:
    • Recency: Days since last purchase
    • Frequency: Number of unique purchases
    • Monetary: Total spend per customer
  • Feature normalization with MinMaxScaler
  • Filtered out 25% of transactions without CustomerID

3. Modeling with K-Means

  • Optimal number of clusters determined with elbow method (K=3)
  • Configuration:
    • Initialization: k-means++
    • Maximum 300 iterations
    • Convergence tolerance: 1e-4
  • Evaluation with multiple validation metrics

Conclusions and Recommendations

Key Findings

  • 67% of customers are valuable (recent purchases, high frequency and spending)
  • 14.4% of customers are inactive and require reactivation strategies
  • Occasional customers (18.6%) represent opportunities to increase frequency
  • The model shows excellent quality according to validation metrics

Recommendations by Segment

Cluster 0: Valuable Customers

  • Exclusive benefits programs (free shipping)
  • Upselling premium products
  • Bundles of complementary products

Cluster 1: Inactive Customers

  • Reactivation campaigns with discounts
  • Satisfaction surveys
  • Cost-benefit evaluation of retention

Cluster 2: Occasional Customers

  • Frequency-based loyalty programs
  • Cross-selling with related products
  • Personalized offers based on history

Future Improvements

  • Categorize products into broader groups for better analysis
  • Include price and discount data for sensitivity analysis
  • Integrate demographic variables for more precise segmentation
