Time Series Forecasting (ARIMA)

Modeling monthly airline passengers (1949-1960)

  • Dataset: Airline Passengers (144 monthly records)
  • Key features: Trend, Seasonality, Autocorrelation
  • Comparative Study: Baseline ARIMA(2,1,2) vs. Seasonal SARIMA(1,1,1)(1,1,1)₁₂ (MAPE: 3.68% | -61% MAE)
  • Technologies: Python, statsmodels, pandas, matplotlib

Project Context

Analysis of the classic airline passengers time series (1949-1960) using ARIMA models for forecasting, evaluating stationarity, autocorrelation and optimal parameters.

Main characteristics:

  • Clear trend: Steady increase in passenger counts
  • Annual seasonality: Repeating pattern every 12 months
  • Non-stationarity: Required differencing (d=1)
  • Autocorrelation: Strong patterns in ACF/PACF

Detailed Methodology

1. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

  • Visualization of trend and seasonality
  • Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) test for stationarity
  • ACF and PACF plots to identify AR/MA parameters
  • Differencing to achieve stationarity

2. ARIMA modeling

  • Parameter selection (p,d,q) based on ACF/PACF
  • Fitting ARIMA(2,1,2) model
  • Validation of residual assumptions
  • Evaluation with metrics: MAE, RMSE, MAPE

Model Benchmarking: Baseline ARIMA vs. Seasonal SARIMA

Comparative evaluation on the 12-month held-out test horizon (1960). While non-seasonal ARIMA(2,1,2) captures local autoregression, it fails to forecast the recurring summer travel surge. Integrating a 12-month seasonal difference and SAR/SMA terms reduces MAE from 41.83 to 16.32 (-61.0%), reduces RMSE from 55.22 to 21.19 (-61.6%), cuts MAPE to 3.68%, and improves AIC by 324.2 points.

Key Visualizations

Autocorrelation (ACF and PACF):

ACF interpretation:

  • Shows significant correlations when bars exceed the shaded area
  • Nearby time points exhibit strong relation (first lags highlighted)
  • Repeating pattern every 12 months indicates annual seasonality
  • Gradual decay in bars indicates decreasing influence of older observations

PACF interpretation:

  • Isolates direct relationships between values by removing intermediate effects
  • Significant peaks at lag 1 (previous month) and lag 12 (same month previous year)
  • Other bars inside the shaded area indicate non-significant correlations
  • Confirms the strongest dependence is with the immediate prior observation and the annual equivalent

Key Findings

  • The series required differencing (d=1) to achieve stationarity
  • Clear seasonal pattern with annual periodicity (12 months)
  • AR(2) and MA(2) parameters were significant
  • Residuals without autocorrelation but slight heteroscedasticity
  • Good predictive performance (MAPE 8.22%)

Conclusions and Recommendations

Model results

  • ARIMA(2,1,2) showed a good fit to the data
  • Significant AR and MA coefficients (p < 0.05)
  • Residuals approximately normally distributed (Shapiro-Wilk p=0.065)
  • Acceptable prediction accuracy (MAPE 8.22%)

Limitations

  • Heteroscedasticity in residuals (non-constant variance)
  • Seasonal pattern not fully modeled

Future improvements

  • Try SARIMA models to capture seasonality
  • Experiment with logarithmic transformations
  • Validate using walk-forward validation techniques
  • Compare with other models (ETS, Neural Networks)
